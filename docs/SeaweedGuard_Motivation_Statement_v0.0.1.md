# Africa’s Blue Futures Hackathon 2027 — Motivation Statement

The future of Africa’s blue economy depends not only on understanding its challenges but also on our ability to translate scientific knowledge into practical, accessible and sustainable solutions. This conviction motivates my application to Africa’s Blue Futures Hackathon 2027.

As a Master’s student in Chemistry at the University of Dar es Salaam, my research interests lie at the intersection of computational chemistry, machine learning, data science and scientific software development. My academic and research experience has strengthened my ability to analyse complex scientific problems, develop computational models, evaluate experimental data and transform research findings into practical digital tools. I am particularly interested in extending these capabilities beyond conventional chemical research to address environmental and socioeconomic challenges.

My first challenge preference is microplastic contamination in farmed seaweed. Seaweed farming sustains thousands of coastal livelihoods in Tanzania, particularly those of women. Protecting the quality of this valuable marine resource therefore represents both an environmental responsibility and an economic opportunity.

Inspired by this challenge, I initiated the development of **SeaweedGuard**, a web-based computational platform that supports microplastic identification through infrared spectral analysis. Its development involved experimental data curation, computational modelling, spectral preprocessing and external validation using 3,832 experimental spectra. The platform achieved 98.21% precision among accepted polymer assignments in the external evaluation. Through this initiative, I have established a computational foundation for translating spectroscopic measurements into actionable evidence for laboratory-supported seaweed quality monitoring.

Beyond this particular challenge, my expertise in chemical modelling, spectroscopy and machine learning can contribute to the development of sustainable materials for seaweed farming and intelligent approaches to seafood quality assessment. I would bring to my team analytical thinking, Python programming, computational experimentation, scientific validation and the ability to develop functional prototypes within an interdisciplinary environment.

I am equally motivated by the opportunity to learn from researchers with expertise in marine science, aquaculture, engineering and entrepreneurship. I believe that meaningful innovation emerges when different disciplines converge around a shared problem, combining scientific understanding with technological creativity and knowledge of local communities.

The hackathon represents an opportunity to advance my commitment to translational research: moving beyond generating scientific knowledge towards developing solutions that create tangible environmental, economic and social value.

I want to participate not simply to present what I already know, but to collaborate, challenge conventional approaches and contribute to innovations that strengthen Africa’s blue economy and the communities whose futures depend on it.
