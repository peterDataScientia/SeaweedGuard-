# SeaweedGuard: Stakeholder and Market Validation Report

**Version:** v0.0.1 | 27 November 2026  
**Status:** Desk research completed; primary stakeholder validation pending  
**Companions:** [Concept note v0.0.8](SeaweedGuard_Concept_Note_v0.0.8.md) · [Scientific validation report v0.0.1](SeaweedGuard_Scientific_Validation_Report_v0.0.1.md)

## Executive summary

Zanzibar is a credible initial setting for investigating a shared laboratory-supported microplastic screening service. Official production statistics show 20,074.6 tonnes of seaweed in 2025, and the United Nations reported more than 23,000 farmers across more than 80 coastal villages in July 2026, approximately 80% of them women. These figures describe the production sector, not demand for SeaweedGuard. Published evidence of coastal microplastics and ongoing quality-management initiatives justifies stakeholder discovery, but does not establish farmed-seaweed contamination prevalence, buyer demand or willingness to pay.

The recommended initial service is laboratory-to-processor/cooperative monitoring, with laboratories as primary operators and producer groups as beneficiaries. No interviews, quotes, customer commitments or partnerships have been obtained for this report. A go/no-go decision should follow direct problem validation, a laboratory feasibility check and a funded local pilot.

## 4. Competition and positioning

| Alternative | Demonstrated capability | Implication for SeaweedGuard |
|---|---|---|
| Open Specy 1.0 (2025) | Free open-source FTIR/Raman analysis, automated processing, ML classifiers and >40,000 spectra | Strong free alternative; avoid claiming software novelty or charging simply for matching. |
| siMPle | Automated microplastic FTIR imaging analysis | Relevant for high-throughput laboratory workflows. |
| Instrument vendor software and manual expert matching | Existing laboratory workflow category; local configuration not established | Measure time, accuracy, review workload and interoperability against current practice. |
| SGS marine monitoring | Advertised microplastic analysis services | Compare actual seaweed-matrix scope, turnaround and cost; not assume local availability. |
| SeaweedGuard MVP | Selective ATR screening, documented E18 performance, review flags and session-based farm observations | Proposed seaweed-oriented coordination and reporting; narrow target coverage and local validation gaps remain. |

The differentiator to test is an integrated, accessible service connecting sampling, laboratory evidence, review and farm feedback. It is not an exclusive algorithmic capability. The scientific report documents 98.21% accepted precision with 63.76% target coverage in E18, not a head-to-head comparison with competing software on external seaweed samples. PE is currently non-target and requires priority investigation given regional sediment evidence.

## 5. Service model and bottom-up market sizing

Define the billable unit as a reportable analysed sample or agreed batch, not an individual spectrum, farm or tonne of seaweed. A test batch must have a documented sampling plan, validated particle-size range, laboratory analysis and qualified interpretation.

- Total addressable market (TAM): sum of annual relevant testing requirements across eligible seaweed buyers/monitoring organisations × verified price per test unit.
- Serviceable available market (SAM): subset within validated analytical scope, geographical reach, budget and accessible laboratory capacity.
- Serviceable obtainable market (SOM): confirmed participating customers × agreed testing frequency, bounded by actual throughput.

None can currently be quantified reliably. The following sensitivity calculation is illustrative only and is not a forecast, measured demand, quoted price or proposed tariff.

| Illustrative participating accounts | Reportable samples/account/month | Annual samples | Annual gross service receipts at hypothetical TZS 50,000/sample |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 2 | 72 | 3,600,000 |
| 5 | 4 | 240 | 12,000,000 |
| 10 | 6 | 720 | 36,000,000 |

Actual price may differ substantially from the illustrative input. Receipts include laboratory service turnover and are not SeaweedGuard profit. Total cost per reportable sample = (collection + transport + extraction + staff + instrument + QA + rework + overhead + computing) / reportable samples. If the verified willingness to pay is below full cost, institutional subsidy, pooled sampling or a narrower validated service may be necessary. Do not shift untested costs to small-scale farmers.

## 6. Primary validation protocol

A purposive discovery sample of 20 conversations is proposed: 8 farmers/producer representatives (seek meaningful participation of women), 4 processors/aggregators, 4 laboratory/research staff, 2 extension/cooperative representatives and 2 standards/public-sector representatives. This is a qualitative discovery exercise, not a representative survey or statistical prevalence study. Obtain voluntary consent, institutional permissions where applicable and permission before recording. Store anonymised results privately; do not publish identifiable interviews or commercially sensitive pricing.

### Common questions (ask before presenting the prototype)

1. Describe your current quality-check process and the last actual quality problem you encountered.
2. Which contamination issues are tested, at what stage and by whom?
3. What evidence triggers an action, rejection or additional test?
4. What does your current process cost and how long does it take? Can you share a non-sensitive example?
5. Is microplastic contamination a recognised concern, an untested concern or not a current priority?
6. Who controls the testing budget, and who benefits from the results?
7. What would make you decline an additional test or digital tool?

### Role-specific probes

| Respondent | Questions |
|---|---|
| Farmers and producer groups | Which practices or materials are difficult to change? How should findings be communicated? Would costs or travel exclude participation? |
| Processors/exporters | What buyer specifications and documented quality incidents exist? How many batches are tested? Would you fund a defined trial, under what conditions? |
| Laboratory staff | Instrument type, ATR accessory, practical minimum particle size, wavenumber range, export format, preparation protocol, staff hours, cost, current matching tools and review criteria? |
| Public/standards bodies | What requirements apply to particular seaweed products and markets? What evidence is necessary to use results for quality decisions? |

After the problem interview, demonstrate an explicitly labelled replay or an authorised live analysis, then observe a participant completing a relevant task. Ask what decision the output would enable, which evidence is missing, whether the existing alternative is preferable and what would justify a pilot. Do not present rejection as absence of plastics. Capture negative evidence.

## 7. Decision gates and action plan

| Gate | Proposed minimum evidence before proceeding |
|---|---|
| Problem relevance | Independent processor and producer accounts of a concrete quality decision where microplastic evidence could help; otherwise reassess priority. |
| Laboratory feasibility | At least one laboratory confirms suitable equipment, export format, staff access and written indicative service cost. |
| Commercial feasibility | At least one budget-holder expresses written conditional interest in a defined pilot; compare documented willingness to pay with full cost. |
| Analytical readiness | Blinded local matrix recovery and identification protocol agreed; PE coverage, model licensing and validation limitations addressed. |
| Operational readiness | Demonstrated sample traceability, operator task completion, qualified reporting and privacy arrangements. |

These are proposed practical gates, not achieved targets. Before the circulated application deadline of 15 December 2026, prioritise initial outreach, two or more producer/buyer conversations, one laboratory meeting and an indicative quotation. During the hackathon, broaden interviews and confirm pilot design. Do not submit the above as completed stakeholder validation. The official challenge catalogue and precise submission requirements remain unverified.

## 8. Conclusion

Desk research supports selecting Zanzibar for customer discovery and developing a shared laboratory service rather than a farmer-paid standalone application. The sector is substantial and there are credible institutional entry points. The commercial opportunity remains a hypothesis until actual monitoring demand, technical feasibility, operating costs and budget-holder interest are established. The next deliverable is a pilot implementation plan and budget informed by laboratory quotations and interview evidence.
